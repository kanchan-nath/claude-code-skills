## 1. Example A: News to Action

**Raw input (summary):** A report says the AI bubble burst in China. If it happens in the US too, will the risk of AI replacing jobs reduce or increase? Nobody knows. Use skills from tutorials and projects to create opportunity. Creator asked ChatGPT which jobs are at highest risk, then decided to build a business automation AI platform that does tasks on behalf of humans.

**Output (about 125 words):**

**Hook**
Recently, a report claimed the AI bubble has burst in China. Now think, what if this happens everywhere? In the US too, with Microsoft, Google, Amazon?

**Question**
Will the risk of AI replacing jobs go down, or will it go up?

**Honest answer**
Honestly, nobody knows the outcome. But I know one thing: waiting won't help.

**Experience**
So when this news came, I didn't panic. I opened ChatGPT and asked, "Which jobs are at the highest risk of being lost?" It gave me a list, and I noticed that most of those tasks were repetitive and could be automated.

**Action**
That's when I decided to build a business automation AI platform. It does the tasks on behalf of humans, so people can focus on the work that actually matters.

**Takeaway**
Whatever you've learnt from tutorials and projects, use it now. Take help from AI, research what digital product you can build, and create your own opportunity.

Because when jobs are uncertain, skills plus action become your safety net.

Why it works:
- The hook is a news fact plus a "what if", so the viewer is already worried.
- The question opens the loop; the takeaway closes it.
- "Honestly, nobody knows" builds trust and avoids false claims.
- The experience block is first-person and specific.
- The last line gives one memorable idea: skills plus action.

## 2. Example B: Pain to Fix

**Raw input (summary):** Everyone knows ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, DeepSeek, Gemini, Antigravity. But 16 other AI agents exist, to use when token limit ends in Claude or Antigravity. Some are free-limit, some paid. A list of 22 best AI coding tools is linked. Using this, creator never stops when tokens run out. CTA: tell which you will use next, subscribe.

**Output (about 115 words):**

**Hook**
You've heard of ChatGPT, Claude, GitHub Copilot, DeepSeek, Gemini, Antigravity. But did you know there are 16 more AI coding agents out there?

**Problem**
You know that moment when your token limit gets over in Claude or Antigravity, right in the middle of building something? Everything just stops.

**Experience**
That used to happen to me too. Then I found these 16 other tools. Some give a free limit, and some are completely paid.

**Solution**
Now, whenever my token limit is exceeded, I simply switch to another agent and keep building. No breaks, no waiting.

**Resource**
I found this list of 22 best AI coding tools for developers and beginners. The link is in the description.

**Takeaway**
So whenever you start your next project, you won't get stuck because of a token limit.

Now tell me in the comments, which tool will you use first? And subscribe, so you don't miss the next one.

Why it works:
- Hook starts from what the viewer knows, then surprises with "16 more".
- The pain moment is vivid ("Everything just stops").
- "That used to happen to me too" connects the pain to the creator's experience.
- The link becomes "in the description"; the URL is never read aloud.
- The CTA asks a question first (comment), then subscribes with a reason.

## 4. What the examples show

- Both scripts open with a hook that needs no context.
- Both give a first-person experience before the solution.
- Both end with one clear takeaway and one CTA.
- Both stay near 120 words, which lands under one minute at normal speaking pace.
- Neither invents facts; every tool, number and claim came from the creator's raw text.
