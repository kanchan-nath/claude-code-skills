# Indian English Voice Guide

Goal: the script should sound like a confident Indian creator talking to a friend or a junior. Clear, warm, direct. Not stiff, not foreign-sounding, not exaggerated.

## Contents
1. Core principles
2. Rhythm and sentence length
3. Natural fillers and connectors
4. Words and phrasing: prefer and avoid
5. Spelling and style
6. Keeping the creator's own voice
7. When the user asks for Hinglish

## 1. Core principles

- Simple words over fancy words. A college student should understand every line on first listen.
- Speak, do not present. Contractions are fine ("don't", "I'm", "that's").
- Be direct and honest. Indian creators connect through "I tried this, this is what happened".
- Keep warmth without gimmicks. No fake hype, no shouting phrases.

## 2. Rhythm and sentence length

- Most sentences: 6-14 words.
- Mix one longer line with one very short line for rhythm. ("Everything just stops.")
- Break lines where a person would take a breath.
- Avoid stacking three clauses with commas. Split them.

## 3. Natural fillers and connectors

Use sparingly, one or two per script, never in the hook:

- "Honestly," "See," "Basically," "Right?" "Now," "So," "And that's when," "Because," "Simple."
- Question tags: "Sounds familiar, right?", "Makes sense, no?" (use lightly)

Do not overuse "guys". Avoid starting with it.

## 4. Words and phrasing

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "Honestly, nobody knows." | "It remains uncertain." |
| "Whatever you've learnt..." | "Utilise your acquired competencies..." |
| "Use it now." | "Leverage it immediately." |
| "I got stuck." | "I encountered a blocker." |
| "Some are free, some are paid." | "A mix of freemium and premium models." |
| "Let me tell you what I did." | "Allow me to elaborate on my methodology." |
| "Switch to another tool." | "Pivot to an alternative solution." |

Also avoid:
- American slang that sounds borrowed ("bro, this is insane, no cap", "game-changer", "lowkey").
- Over-formal or textbook phrases ("in today's rapidly evolving landscape").
- Marketing jargon ("synergy", "unlock", "supercharge") unless the creator used it.

## 5. Spelling and style

- Use British/Indian spelling where it comes naturally: "learnt", "organise", "colour", "programme" (but keep tool and product names exactly as written).
- "Fresher", "college", "job", "skills", "tutorial", "project" are all natural.
- Numbers: write small numbers as words when spoken ("sixteen"), but digits are fine on the page for clarity. Keep what reads best aloud.
- Normal capitalisation and standard punctuation in the written script.

## 6. Keeping the creator's own voice

- Reuse the creator's own good phrases. If they said "I show you how I think and proceed", keep the spirit: "Let me show you how I think."
- Do not make the script sound smoother than the creator realistically speaks.
- Keep their stance: if they sound humble and practical, stay humble and practical.
- Keep first-person experience. Never swap "I" for "we" unless the creator did.

## 7. When the user asks for Hinglish

- Keep the structure identical; only change the language mix.
- Use Roman script Hinglish unless asked for Devanagari.
- Keep technical terms in English (token limit, AI agent, project).
- Example: "Jab AI bubble ke burst hone ki news aayi, maine panic nahi kiya. Maine ChatGPT se pucha, kaunsi jobs sabse zyada risk mein hain."
