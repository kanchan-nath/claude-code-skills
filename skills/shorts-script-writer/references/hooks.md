# Hook Formulas

The hook is the first 1-3 seconds (about 8-15 spoken words). It must work even if the viewer has zero context. The first frame and the first spoken line should say the same thing, so on-screen text can mirror it.

## Contents
1. What makes a hook work
2. Formulas with examples
3. Hooks to avoid
4. How to pick one

## 1. What makes a hook work

- It names the viewer's world (their tool, their fear, their goal) in the first few words.
- It opens a gap: something the viewer wants closed.
- It is specific. "16 more AI coding agents" beats "many more tools".
- It is spoken-friendly. Read it aloud; if you stumble, rewrite it.

## 2. Formulas with examples

**Familiar-then-surprise** (list what they know, then reveal what they don't)
- "You know ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini. But there are 16 more AI coding tools you've never tried."

**News-then-"what if"** (current event, then the bigger fear)
- "A report says the AI bubble has burst in China. What if that happens in the US too?"

**Pain moment** (a frustration the viewer has lived)
- "You're mid-project, and suddenly your Claude limit is over. Sound familiar?"

**Direct promise** (exact payoff and who it is for)
- "If you're a fresher worried about AI taking jobs, watch this till the end."

**Contrarian claim** (a belief most would push back on)
- "Learning one more tutorial won't save your job. Here's what actually will."

**Specific number**
- "I found 16 AI coding tools that work when your token limit ends."

**Question the viewer must answer in their head**
- "Will AI take more jobs, or fewer, if the bubble bursts?"

**Quick story open**
- "Last week, my Claude limit ended in the middle of building. I almost gave up. Then this happened."

## 3. Hooks to avoid

- "Hey guys, welcome back to my channel..."
- "Today I am going to tell you about..."
- Anything that needs a full sentence of setup before the point.
- Clickbait the script cannot pay off. The ending must satisfy the hook.
- Vague superlatives ("This will change everything").

## 4. How to pick one

1. Find the most surprising, specific or emotional fact in the raw script.
2. Put it first, in one short line.
3. If the raw script has a number, use it.
4. If the raw script has a fear or frustration, use that as a question or pain moment.
5. Check that the last line of the script answers or pays off the hook.
