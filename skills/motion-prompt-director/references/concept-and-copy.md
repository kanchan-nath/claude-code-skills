## 1. What a concept is

Three sentences, written before any prompt:

| Part | Question | Example (Tessel, a calendar app) |
|---|---|---|
| Idea | What should the viewer believe afterwards? (8 words max) | Your week doesn't fit. Tessel fits it. |
| Device | What ONE thing carries the idea through every shot? | A red "now" dot handed from shot to shot, ending as the logo's dot |
| Grammar | What rule does the film's FORM obey? | Everything fits together with no gaps; opens and closes on the same tick |

A concept is NOT a style ("bold kinetic minimal 3D"), a feature list, a mood ("premium, energetic"), or a format. If you cannot fill Grammar, the film will look like everyone else's.

## 2. Inventory before ideas

Fill these from the brief before ideating. Generic inputs make generic films. Fill the first two rows first.

| List | Prompt |
|---|---|
| Name | What it means or evokes (etymology, second meaning, the physical thing it names). The name often hands you the device and the mark. |
| Non-obvious behaviour | Finish: "Unlike the obvious version, it ..." (splits by item not evenly; ranks not lists; reschedules only the future). This is the proof beat. |
| Value sentence | "Before: ___. After: ___." in the viewer's words, no feature names. |
| Verbs | 10 verbs the product performs, literal to abstract. |
| Product objects | Things on its screen with their states (a row that resolves, a card that folds, a reading that crosses a threshold). The device is usually one of these. |
| The number | The one quantity that changes (412 to 1, 42% to 18%). A number can carry a whole film. |
| Pain scene | Time, place, what is on screen at the worst moment. The hook is shot here. |
| Category cliches | The five images every film in this category uses. Banned for this film. |
| World sound | What the product's world sounds like (a tick, a ping, a pour, a latch). |

## 3. Three lenses (nine raw ideas, one finalist per lens)

The first idea per lens is usually the cliche; the third is usually yours. Start from the smart part (non-obvious behaviour) and make it visible.

- (a) **Verb made literal.** Take a verb, ask what physical process it names, stage it using the product's own objects as the material. Fail: a cartoon metaphor beside a screenshot.
- (b) **Pain made visible.** Give the pain a physical quantity (mass, height, noise, count) and let it grow until the frame cannot hold it; the turn relieves the same quantity. Fail: a stock sad person; a problem stated in text; a problem act over ~20% of the film.
- (c) **Formal device.** Relay object (one thing handed shot to shot), bookend (same image first and last, one thing changed), one take (single unbroken camera move), container becomes product, counter as spine, split-sync (before/after on the same beat), rule of the frame (only vertical moves; everything gets quieter), scale jump (16 px vs full frame).

When all nine are weak: make the invisible visible; follow one unit through its life; invert (show the absence); borrow a physical law (gravity, pressure, tessellation); make the logo guilty (build the mark from the problem's pieces); constrain (no cuts / only three objects / one colour change); oppose the category.

## 4. Tests that kill weak ideas

| Test | Run it | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | Swap in a competitor's name; strike every prop another product could use | Film still works for the competitor; fewer than two owned props remain |
| Deletion | Delete the demo beats: value must still read. Delete the value beats: film must stop working | Value vanishes with the demo, or film "works" without value (it was a tour) |
| Muted | Describe it with pictures only | Problem to result cannot be told from the images |
| Feature word | Would a stranger, muted, use the brief's word for the feature? | They would name a neighbouring thing; keep the feature's familiar form and let the device dress it |
| Poster | Describe the last frame as a wall poster | It does not say who made it and what it does |
| One sentence | Say the idea aloud | You need "and" (two ideas) or an adjective (a style) |
| Retell | How would a viewer describe it to a friend? | The answer is "it was slick", not an image |
| Specificity | Imagine a simpler product (even split vs itemised; list vs ranking). Is the film equally true of it? | Yes: the proof shows the naive version; rebuild around the non-obvious behaviour |
| Budget | Count capabilities and beats | More than 2 capabilities or more than 10 beats per 30 s |
| Truth | Do numbers and states agree with the story in every frame? | The payer shown owing; a "sorted" list not sorted; a figure appearing twice |

**Generic props that fail ownership** unless the product literally is the thing: rocket, lightbulb, globe with arcs, up-and-right chart, phone/laptop floating in a void, glowing orb or sparkle for AI, shield/padlock for security, puzzle pieces for integration, gears for automation, stopwatch for speed, magnifying glass for search, confetti for success, handshake, bullseye.

Logline = device + what it does + the turn + the result, 15 words max, no "and" joining two ideas. When the user wants to choose: show three loglines (device + one frame each) with a recommendation in 12 lines.

## 5. The story device

Sources, in order of preference: (1) the product's own object with states; (2) the name's meaning; (3) the key number; (4) a primitive of the mark; (5) the accent colour; (6) a sound (only as a partner). A bare dot/line/block is the lazy default (it is Tessel's device) - use one only if it gets behaviour only this product has.

A good device is: **one** (single instance at a time); able to **change scale** (travels as a small thing, fills the frame once); has a **state that changes** (ticks, fills, drops, locks, counts); **owned**; and **ends as the mark** (launch/sting) or as the unit the product acts on in its resolved state (loop/product video).

Write the path as a chain of "becomes", one link per shot, naming the seam type. Rules: never blinks out (full opacity through every seam); changes role, not style, at a seam; the eye is already where the cut lands; at most one flood (usually the turn); lock the target before landing.

Bookend: open and close on the same image and sound with exactly one thing changed - the change is the promise (clock 3:12 a.m. to 3:13 a.m.).

## 6. Beats

| Beat | Job | Length | Works when | Fails as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | Inside the problem, already moving at frame 0 | 1-2 s | problem felt by 1 s, stated by 2 s | logo, "Introducing", a question, blank frame |
| Problem | Escalate the pain as a visible quantity | 2-4 s | a stranger names the pain muted | a statement with no picture |
| Turn | Device/product intervenes; biggest change so far, on a downbeat | 1-2 s | strongest visual event yet (flood, drop, implosion, silence then drop) | the product just appears |
| Proof | One capability as cause > action > result | 4-8 s each, 1-2 per film | result holds 0.6 s + 0.1 s per word before the camera leaves | a feature list |
| Promise | Idea stated once as a callback to the setup words | 2-4 s | reuses a word from the hook | an unrelated slogan |
| Lockup | Device becomes the mark; name; URL if needed | 3-5 s with tail | final frame works as the poster | replayed reveal; fade to black |

**Cause > action > result.** Cause: something visible makes the action necessary (a typed intent, an arriving event, an accumulated mess, a threshold crossed). Action: the product's real behaviour in its own UI, no sparkle standing in for "magic". Result: a changed state still true when paused (a counter that counted, a list now in order).

## 7. Arcs by format

| Format | Arc | Where the idea lands |
|---|---|---|
| Launch film 20-45 s | hook > problem > turn > proof x1-2 > promise > lockup | turn at ~20-25%, callback at the promise |
| Product/feature video 8-30 s | situation > cause > action > result (held) > name | the result |
| Feature loop 4-15 s | rest A > cause > action > result > rest A | mid-loop; last frame = first |
| Logo sting 3-8 s | gather > assemble with a contact > hit > wordmark > building hold | the hit |
| UI walkthrough 20-90 s | cold open on end result (2-4 s) > chapters (cause > action > result > recap) > lockup | each chapter's result |
| Teaser <= 15 s | the effect without the product > name and date | the name |
| Before/after | same shot twice, identical camera move: without, then with | the difference |

30 s launch proportions: hook+problem ~20%, turn ~10%, proof ~35-40%, promise ~15%, lockup+tail ~15-20%.

## 8. End cards

1. The device becomes the mark (the lockup arrives from the story, not as a new logo animation).
2. Assemble over ~1-1.3 s, then hold ~0.6-1.3 s still, or keep building (slow 10-20% push). Total 1.5-2.5 s. A static card sitting 2.75 s reads dead.
3. URL / date / "out now": readable at least 1.7 s, secondary size, the ONLY line besides the name. If the brief gives a practical fact it must be there. With none, a launch film still says what the product is in one short line under the name; a bare name is for stings.
4. The final frame is the poster: it must contain the mark and the name.
5. Tail of at least 1 s while sound decays to silence. Never fade to black.
6. No row of social icons, store badges, second tagline.

## 9. Copy

**Budget (words in the film's own voice; UI strings the product shows are separate):**

| Format | Words | Per line | Per shot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch film, 30 s | aim 15-25, cap 35 | 5 (~24 characters) | 2 lines max, one idea |
| Product / feature video | 15 | 5 | 1 line |
| Feature loop | 8, reads muted | 4 | 1 line |
| Logo sting | name + 4 | 4 | 1 line |
| UI walkthrough | 6 per caption | 6 | 1 caption |

A viewer reads ~3 words per second while watching motion.

**Voice:** the viewer's words at the moment of pain ("Looks good to me." "Not again."). Nouns, verbs, numbers; no praise adjectives; a number beats an adjective. Present tense, active; the viewer's world is the subject ("Meetings move over." not "Tessel moves your meetings."). Sentence case, period at the end, no exclamation marks, questions, emoji or ellipses.

**The line test (all six):** true on its own paused frame; a competitor could not say it; 5 words or fewer; no praise adjective; sounds like a person talking; still true in a year (no "new", "now with").

**Callback copy** - plant early, pay off later, changed by what the film showed:
- Same words, new truth ("Looks good to me." a tired lie at 0:04, true at 0:22).
- One word flips ("Your week doesn't fit." > "Everything fits.").
- The digit ("3:12 a.m." > "3:13 a.m.").
- The user's words (typed "Protect my mornings." > "Mornings stay yours.").
- Grammar rhyme ("18%. Water now." / "42%. Leave it.").
- Tautology as proof ("Spending money stays spending money.").
One main chain per film plus at most one echo. The payoff reuses at least one content word from the setup; never explain the callback on screen.

**Banned (hard):** eyebrow/kicker labels; stacked taglines; "Introducing..."; text walls; filler text (decorative mono captions, fake metrics, version strings, labels nobody needs, lorem ipsum).
**Also cut on sight:** Meet / Say hello to / Ready to...? / What if... / Imagine...; the future of, reimagined, redefined, next-gen, revolutionary, game-changing, like never before; unlock, supercharge, streamline, elevate, empower, level up, harness; seamless(ly), effortless(ly), powerful, smart, intelligent, AI-powered, all-in-one, at scale; workflow, productivity, solution, experience, journey; a feature name as a headline ("Smart Split."); three-item lists ("Plan. Track. Win.").

**String audit (generative tools garble text, so keep it short and overlay in an editor):** plural/zero handled ("No clashes", never "0 clashes"); one brand casing; sentence case; curly quotes; en dash for ranges; one time/currency format; dates match weekdays; invented plausible names (never real people/companies, "John Doe", "Acme", example.com).

## 10. Four condensed worked concepts (all fictional; use as pattern, never copy)

- **Plumb** (code review, 30 s launch). Smart part: ranks 2,184 changed lines and surfaces the 3 that can break production. Device: a steel plumb bob (the name) that drops through the diff and stops dead on the bug; in UI it is the gutter marker. Grammar: everything moves vertically under gravity until the lockup. Callback: "Looks good to me." tired lie > same words, now true. Copy 21 words.
- **Loam** (soil sensor, 8 s muted loop). Smart part: waits for THIS pot to reach THIS plant's threshold, not a schedule. Device: the waterline in a cross-section of one pot; becomes the progress bar, then the underline under "Water now"; line climbs back to rest = frame 0. Copy 6 words: "18%. Water now." > "42%. Leave it."
- **Hush** (on-call tool, 20 s). Smart part: does not mute; groups 412 alerts by shared cause and wakes the one owner. Device: the incident card (born as alert row 1; the other 411 fold into it, count ticking down). Rule: after the turn, the film only gets quieter. Bookend: "3:12 a.m." > "3:13 a.m."
- **Keel** (money app, 45 s walkthrough). Smart part: every paycheck fills the buffer below the baseline first, so a surprise bill drains the buffer, never spending money. Device: one deposit row followed through every chapter. Rule: the camera may tilt, the balance baseline never does. Captions 6 words max.
